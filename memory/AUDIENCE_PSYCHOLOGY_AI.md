## Who Is Actually Watching

Not developers. Not researchers. Not enterprise CTOs.

The core follower is someone aware that AI is changing everything but unsure where
they fit in it. They have a day job. They feel the ground shifting beneath their
career. They follow @CuttingEdgeOfAI because it makes them feel like they're staying
ahead — even when they're not acting on anything yet.

Secondary segment: early adopters who already use AI daily — following for
validation, new tool discovery, and community identity.

Both segments share one core fear: being left behind without knowing it.

Age skew: 24–40. Professional context: knowledge workers, freelancers, creators,
mid-level managers. Comfortable enough to use ChatGPT daily. Not comfortable enough
to build with an API.

---

## The Core Tension (Most Important Section)

This audience lives in a permanent gap between awareness and action.

They know more than they do. They save more than they implement. They follow more
accounts than they change behaviors from. This is not laziness — it's the natural
state of someone watching a fast-moving space while also holding down a real life.

This tension is the engine of the account.

Content that acknowledges this gap without shaming them for it builds loyalty.
Content that says "here's what you should already be doing" alienates them.
Content that says "here's what most people haven't caught up to yet" validates them.

The best performing seeds will live at this intersection:
"You're aware enough to follow this. Here's the one specific thing that closes
the gap between knowing and doing."

GPT instruction: Every seed should implicitly speak to someone who knows about AI
but hasn't fully acted on it yet. The tone is never "you're behind." It's always
"you're actually ahead — here's the next step."

## Core Emotional Drivers

### 1. Displacement Anxiety
Fear that AI will quietly make their skills irrelevant while they're still showing
up and doing their job. They don't need to be told AI is coming. They need to know
what to DO this week, specifically.

Content angle: Anchor every news seed to a practical consequence. Never let
information end at the information. End at the implication.

### 2. Identity as an Early Adopter
Following this account is a signal they send to themselves: I'm the kind of person
who pays attention. Content that makes them feel ahead gets saved and shared.
Content that makes them feel behind gets scrolled past — even if it's accurate.

Content angle: Frame information as insider knowledge, not education.
"Most people don't know this yet" lands harder than "Here's how AI works."

### 3. Productivity as Status
Being efficient is a flex in this world. Knowing which tools to use, how to stack
them, how to cut hours off a workflow — that's currency they share like fitness wins.

Content angle: Name the tools. Name the time saved. Name the specific task.
"This workflow saves 3 hours on X" beats "AI can improve your productivity."

### 4. FOMO as the Save Trigger
Most follows are curiosity-driven. Most saves are FOMO-driven. They save carousels
they haven't implemented because knowing it feels like protection.

Content angle: The save is the conversion. Final slides should justify the save:
"You'll want this later" / "Bookmark before this changes" /
"Most people figure this out too late."

### 5. The Scroll-Stop Trigger
Before any of the above applies, the cover slide has to stop the scroll.
For this audience, scroll-stop happens when specificity collides with surprise.

What stops them:
- A number they didn't expect
- A consequence they haven't considered
- A comparison that resolves a real current debate
- A contrarian frame on something they assumed was settled

What doesn't stop them:
- "AI is changing everything" — seen 400 times
- Vague superlatives ("The most powerful tool yet")
- Anything that sounds like a press release headline

GPT instruction: Every seed must have a natural scroll-stop hook implicit in it.
If you cannot identify the surprising or specific element, discard the seed.

## Contrarian Content — Defined for This Niche

"Contrarian" in the AI space is mostly fake. True contrarian means pushing back on
a narrative the majority of AI accounts are currently promoting, with a specific reason.

Real contrarian for this niche:
- "GPT-4o is not the best model for [specific use case]"
- "AI prompt engineering is dying — here's what's replacing it"
- "The tool everyone's recommending is slower than the one nobody talks about"
- "Why automating your email is actually hurting your response rates"

Fake contrarian (never generate):
- "AI isn't perfect and here's why" — no stakes, no surprise
- "You still need human creativity" — safe, generic, predictable
- "Not everything needs AI" — obvious, no new information

True contrarian seeds must cite a specific tool, model, workflow, or industry claim
being pushed back on. If there is no strong current target this month, do not force
contrarian seeds to hit the quota. Reallocate those slots to Insider or Breaking News.

## Voice and Tone — Two Modes

### Cover / Caption Voice
Used for: carousel cover slides, first caption line, CTA slides.
Register: bold, slightly provocative, confidence without arrogance.

Phrases that fit:
- "Here's what actually changed"
- "Most people are sleeping on this"
- "This is the part nobody's talking about"
- "This dropped quietly and it's bigger than it looks"
- "If you're still doing X, stop. Here's why."
- "Nobody asked but the answer is [tool/workflow]"

Phrases that don't fit:
- "Revolutionary" / "game-changing" — overused, low effort signal
- "In today's fast-paced world" — instant credibility kill
- "Did you know?" — condescending to this audience
- "The future is here" — vague, no stakes

### Inner Slide Voice
Used for: body slides 2 through N-1 in a carousel.
Register: declarative, evidence-forward, punchy. One idea per slide.
No rhetorical questions. No padding. State the thing, support it briefly, move on.

Examples of good inner slide copy:
- "Claude outperforms GPT-4o on multi-step reasoning tasks by 23% in independent
  benchmarks. Most people are still defaulting to GPT out of habit."
- "The workflow: Perplexity for research → Claude for synthesis → Notion for output.
  Total time: 40 minutes. Without AI: half a day."

## Content That Performs vs. Dies

### Performs
- Breaking news + a "so what" consequence angle
- "X things [specific tool] can do that most people don't know" with real examples
- Named workflow: specific tools, steps, and time savings
- True contrarian with a specific current target
- Consequence framing with a near-term timeframe
- Head-to-head comparisons resolving a real current decision
- Anything that feels like early access to information

### Dies
- "AI is changing the world" with no specific hook
- Pure news recap with no opinion or implication
- Tool lists with no context on why each matters for a specific use case
- Motivational framing — signals low quality to this audience immediately
- Anything postable in any month this year without changing a word
- Content requiring developer-level knowledge to understand or act on

## Seed Bank Content Categories

Six buckets. Assign every seed to exactly one. Distribution is a guide — adjust
based on what happened in the current month. If it was a heavy release month,
weight Breaking News higher. If it was a quiet month, weight Workflow and Insider.

| Category | Target % | Seeds | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breaking News + Angle | 25% | ~7-8 | Real news from current month + consequence frame. Never just the news. |
| Tool Workflows | 20% | ~6 | Named tools, named steps, named outcome. No vague productivity talk. |
| Head-to-Head Comparisons | 15% | ~4-5 | Two tools/models resolving a real current decision. |
| True Contrarian | 10% | 2-3 if targets exist | Pushes back on a specific popular narrative. Skip if no strong target. |
| Insider / Early Signal | 15% | ~4-5 | Feels like early access. Not widely known yet. |
| Consequence Framing | 15% | ~4-5 | "If you're not doing X by [timeframe], here's what happens." Specific. |

Diversity rule: No two seeds in the same category may share the same hook
structure or name the same company or tool.

Anchor test: Before finalizing each seed, identify: what is the ONE specific
thing that makes this seed only possible in the AI/productivity niche in this
specific month? If you cannot name it, the seed is invalid.

Hook-first rule: The first three words of every seed must contain either a
named entity (tool, company, model name) OR a number OR a surprising verb.
If the first three words are generic, the seed is invalid.

## Banned Content Directions

Never generate seeds in these directions:

- Generic "Top N AI tools" lists with no original angle or current hook
- Pure motivational content without a concrete specific AI hook
- Content requiring developer-level knowledge to understand or act on
- Vague future predictions with no near-term grounding ("By 2030...")
- Fake contrarian (see contrarian section)
- Anything that reads like a reworded press release
- Any seed where swapping "AI" for another topic would make it equally valid
  for a fitness, cooking, or finance account — not specific enough

## Instructions for GPT-4o-mini — Seed Generation Protocol

Read the [CURRENT_MONTH_CONTEXT] block first. Use it to anchor timely seeds.

Then generate 30 seeds following this sequence:

1. Assign each to exactly one category
2. Apply the hook-first rule — first three words: named entity, number, or surprising verb
3. Apply the scroll-stop test — identify the specific surprising element
4. Apply the anchor test — name the one thing that makes this only possible this month
5. Apply the diversity rule — no two seeds in the same category share hook structure or named entity
6. Check contrarian quota — only generate if strong targets exist this month
7. Assign a ManyChat keyword or NONE
8. Cross-reference against posted-stories.json — no topic duplicates
9. Output exactly 30 seeds, numbered

Required format:
[Category] | [Hook-first topic sentence, max 12 words] | [Keyword]

Good seed:
Breaking News + Angle | OpenAI's memory update now knows more about you than you think | BREAKING

Bad seed:
Tool Workflows | How to use AI to be more productive at work | WORKFLOW

The bad seed fails: no named entity in first three words, no scroll-stop hook,
not anchored to current month, applies equally to any productivity account.
